# Deployment of Green Hydrogen-Compatible Industrial Plants will be insufficient to decarbonise Steel, Ammonia and Methanol Production by 2050
by Alice Bennett, Rebecca Peer, Jannik Haas, André Cabrera Serrenho


This paper has been submitted for publication.

# Abstract
Green hydrogen may enable the decarbonisation of sectors which rely on fossil fuels but cannot be directly electrified. Yet, in many of these sectors existing industrial plants will need to be replaced with green hydrogen-compatible alternatives. The impact of continued use of existing industrial plants has been quantified by committed emission analyses, but how limited deployment of green hydrogen-compatible industrial plants will impact a transition to green hydrogen has not yet been explored. In this work, we fill this gap by combining committed emissions analysis with modelling of the deployment of green hydrogen-compatible industrial plants. This is used to assess the global decarbonisation potential of green hydrogen use in three industries: steel, ammonia, and methanol. We show that, although 97% of existing industrial plants are likely to be decommissioned by 2050, if the rate of deployment of hydrogen-compatible industrial plants does not accelerate, green hydrogen use would be limited to 57% of current demand in 2050. Only when green hydrogen-compatible industrial plants are immediately available to replace retired facilities are cumulative emissions found to align with 1.5ºC climate pathways. However, this scenario is unlikely to be feasible, given the limited availability of green hydrogen and constrained renewable energy supply.
